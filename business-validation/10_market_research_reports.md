## OPPORTUNITY A — "Recover": AI revenue-recovery agent for high-ticket service SMBs

1. **Executive summary.** Owner-led, high-ticket appointment/estimate service businesses lose large, *already-earned* revenue to declined estimates, unscheduled treatment, no-shows, and lapsed customers because the front desk never works the list. "Recover" is an AI agent that autonomously runs persistent, personalised, human-approved recovery, priced on contingency. Strongest WTP and fastest paid validation in the study.
2. **Problem.** Invisible revenue leakage; the manual fix is reliably dropped.
3. **Customer.** 1–5-location auto repair / optometry / aesthetic / (dental) practices; owner = buyer = victim.
4. **Pain evidence.** Dental $1M–1.5M unscheduled/provider; optometry 25% no-show + 3–5% manual recall yield at 4.5 hrs/100; auto declined-estimates daily; vet 5–20% unbilled (file 03). *Magnitudes vendor-softened; direction strong.*
5–8. **Market size (conservative, US):**
   - **TAM (directional):** virtual-receptionist/patient-engagement + reactivation tooling is a multi-$B adjacent market (e.g., patient-engagement ~$ low-billions; reactivation a slice). *Treat as directional.*
   - **SAM:** ~200k US independent high-ticket service practices (auto repair ~160k shops; ~40k optometry; ~190k dental practices; med-spas ~10k+) × plausible $3–9k/yr recovery fee ≈ **low-single-digit $B**.
   - **SOM (realistic, solo, year 1):** 20–50 paying accounts × ~$5–10k/yr contingency = **$150k–$400k ARR-equivalent.** This is the number that matters for a solo founder.
9–11. **Competitors / alternatives / complaints.** Weave, RevenueWell, Solutionreach, Podium, Bolt On (reminder-grade, not autonomous outcome-priced recovery); ISAs/VAs; front desk. Complaints: generic blasts, owner must configure, not tied to specific lost-revenue events.
12–13. **WTP / pricing benchmark.** Recall tools $200–500/mo; ISAs $900–1,500/mo; RCM cuts 5–30%. Our offer: 15–25% of recovered revenue, or low SaaS + success fee.
14. **Distribution.** Vertical FB groups, trade forums/conferences, cold outreach, case studies.
15–16. **Regulatory / operational risk.** Low for auto/optometry/aesthetic; **HIPAA for dental** (defer or partner). Messaging-consent (TCPA) compliance required — manageable with opt-out + human approval.
17. **Founder advantage.** AI engineering + business judgement + speed. **(Blockchain: not applicable — and that's fine.)**
18. **Wedge.** Vertical + autonomous + outcome-priced recovery; start where incumbents are weakest (auto/optometry/aesthetic, not dental).
19. **Value proposition.** "We recover the revenue your front desk is too busy to chase — pay only for what we bring back."
20. **Business Model Canvas (compressed).** *Customers:* owner-led high-ticket service SMBs. *Value:* found money, zero-risk. *Channels:* founder-led + community. *Revenue:* contingency → SaaS+success. *Key activities:* AI follow-up engine, per-vertical integrations. *Costs:* LLM/API, founder time. *Key resource:* per-vertical recovery playbooks/data.
21. **Value Proposition Canvas.** *Jobs:* recover lost revenue without spamming customers or hiring. *Pains:* leak, dropped follow-up, spam fear, cost. *Gains:* found money, control, no risk. *Relievers:* contingency, human-in-loop, in-your-voice. *Creators:* "$X recoverable now," recovered-$ dashboard.
22. **Validation decision: GREEN — proceed to concierge paid test.**

## OPPORTUNITY B — Vertical collections (self-storage / associations)

1. **Exec summary.** Recurring-revenue back-offices bleed time and cash to delinquency chasing + compliance (self-storage: 10–15 hrs/mgr/mo at only 3–4% delinquency; 90–120-day lien minefield). Vertical collections automation + compliance.
4. **Pain evidence.** ai-lean/storagepug (storage); i4a (associations); strong frequency.
5–8. **Market.** SAM: ~60k US self-storage facilities + tens of thousands of associations. SOM (solo y1): 20–40 accounts × $3–8k/yr ≈ **$100–300k.**
9. **Competitors.** Horizontal AR (Chaser, Upflow) — not vertical; built-in modules weak.
14. **Distribution.** SelfStorageTalk, ISS, state associations.
15. **Risk.** Lien-law correctness (must be provably right); narrower TAM.
22. **Validation decision: GREEN — strong alternative beachhead.**

## OPPORTUNITY C — Job-shop / field-service quoting

1. **Exec summary.** Small shops lose 7-figure accounts because quotes are slow (Excel + "eyeball"); first quote wins. AI turns an RFQ into a fast first-pass quote (human-approved).
4. **Pain evidence.** practicalmachinist/mmsonline; 96% of effort on a 4%-win bucket; 78% buy from first responder.
5–8. **Market.** SAM: ~50k+ US small machine/fab shops + contractors. SOM (solo y1): 15–30 accounts × $4–10k/yr ≈ **$100–300k.**
9. **Competitors.** Paperless Parts (mid/large, low SMB penetration); Excel.
15. **Risk.** Estimate-accuracy liability; conservative slow buyers; longer build.
22. **Validation decision: GREEN — promising but harder MVP; behind A/B on speed-to-revenue.**

## #4 — Scope-creep capture (project B2B) — summary
Strong pain, only home for founder's verifiable-record skill, but **behavioral kill-risk** (owner must confront client) + harder outcome-attribution. **YELLOW.** Carry as the "founder-skill" option, not the lead.

## Market Research verdict → Business Model (Stage 11)

**Rank after full research:** **A (Recover) > B (Vertical collections) > C (Quoting) > #4 (Scope-creep).** A wins on speed-to-revenue, WTP clarity (contingency), and lowest behavioral barrier. B is the strongest fallback. The three are the *same company* at different beachheads, so the decision is **which vertical to enter first** — a question only real customer conversations can finalise (see Assumption Register, file 18).
