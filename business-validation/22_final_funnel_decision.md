## STAGE C — Online Interview Proxy (top 3, grounded in real evidence)

### Idea 1 — Lost-revenue recovery / win-back (declined estimates, lapsed customers, no-shows)
1. **Complaining about?** Already-earned revenue walking out; front desk never works the call-back list.
2. **Words they use:** "no-shows," "patients who never rebooked," "declined treatment," "deferred estimates," "reactivation," "empty chairs."
3. **How painful?** Chronic + invisible (never shows in the POS as a lost sale) → under-attacked but large in aggregate.
4. **What they do today:** front desk works lists between calls; mailers; recall tools; ISAs/VAs.
5. **Hate about current fixes:** generic blast texts; the list rots when phones ring; ISAs cost $900–1,500/mo regardless of result.
6. **Already paying?** **Yes** — Weave/RevenueWell/Solutionreach/Podium; Bolt On (auto); ISAs/VAs.
7. **Would pay for?** A cut of revenue actually recovered (contingency) — near-frictionless.
8. **Distrust:** "an AI texting MY customers badly hurts my reputation."
9. **Yes to pilot if:** human-approves messages, "in my voice," $0 if nothing recovered.
10. **Strong evidence:** existence of a paid reactivation/recall tool category (WTP); no-show rates (Clearwave; ICO optometry 24.8%); auto declined-approval follow-up (Bolt On). Sources: clerri.com, dentalbilling.com, boltontechnology.com/missed-repair-approvals, clearwaveinc.com, MGMA.
11. **Weak evidence:** the *magnitude* of recoverable $ (dental "$1M–1.5M unscheduled/provider" is **vendor-sourced** — treat as hypothesis).

### Idea 2 — AR / late-invoice collections-as-a-service
1. **Complaining about?** Customers paying late or not at all; hours chasing money.
2. **Words:** "deadbeat," "won't pay," "chasing invoices," "soul-destroying," "net-90," "stiffed."
3. **How painful?** Highest *emotional* intensity in real owner forums; existential cash-flow for small firms.
4. **Today:** reminder emails, late fees, small claims, collection agencies, factoring.
5. **Hate:** chasing is unbillable + relationship-damaging; collections recover pennies (one case: **$250 of $2,500 over 18 months**); agencies take big cuts.
6. **Already paying?** **Yes** — collection agencies, lawyers, factoring/funding companies.
7. **Would pay for?** A cut of recovered cash (contingency).
8. **Distrust:** "a third party chasing MY client will damage the relationship"; fear of looking desperate.
9. **Yes to pilot if:** done in their name, polite, they control tone.
10. **Strong evidence (best *organic*, non-vendor in the whole study):** LawnSite threads — "Clients who don't pay? what to do?" (lawnsite.com/threads/clients-who-dont-pay-what-to-do.413653/), "What to do when a customer won't pay" (…/515754/); mikeholt forums non-payment threads; Ignition 2025 (97% agencies late-paid, 84% spend 3–10+ hrs/mo chasing); BD+C/Rabbet $64B construction; UK Small Business Commissioner case studies.
11. **Weak evidence:** that owners will let an *outsider* chase their clients (trust barrier unproven); recovery rates on already-late debt.

### Idea 3 — Quote-fast for trades / job-shops ("first quote wins")
1. **Complaining about?** Quoting eats nights/weekends; slow quotes lose jobs.
2. **Words:** "free estimates," "ghosted," "first to quote wins," "eyeball guesstimate," "RFQ."
3. **How painful?** Sharp — ~96% of estimating effort on a ~4%-win RFQ bucket; lost 7-figure accounts.
4. **Today:** Excel + "eyeball"; owner after-hours; takeoff services; Paperless Parts (mid/large).
5. **Hate:** speed-vs-accuracy trade; conservative buyers distrust auto-quotes.
6. **Already paying?** Some (estimators, Paperless Parts) — low SMB penetration.
7. **Would pay for?** Faster accurate first-pass quotes — but **flat SaaS** (contingency hard: can't attribute "won because faster").
8. **Distrust:** "a wrong quote costs me money" (liability).
9. **Yes to pilot if:** human approves every number.
10. **Strong evidence:** practicalmachinist + mmsonline threads; first-responder dynamic.
11. **Weak evidence:** that it can be **validated without building** (you must produce real quotes → needs tooling); attribution of value.

### Why #1 (win-back) beats #2 (AR collections) — decisive, evidence-based
1. **Regulation:** Win-back is **marketing to a business's own past/declined customers** → no debt-collection regime; only TCPA consent. AR collections-as-a-service makes you a **third-party debt collector** → **state licensing/registration** (CA DCLA, FL OFR) and **FDCPA** exposure on any consumer debt. Clear rule-violation risk ("low regulation," "testable in 7–30 days").
2. **Trust barrier:** Owners fear an outsider chasing *their clients* will damage relationships (loud in the forums). Win-back has the *opposite* emotional valence — friendly "we miss you / your quote's still good," not "pay up."
3. **Attribution & self-funding:** Both are contingency, but win-back's recovered bookings are cleanly attributable in the calendar/POS; collections on aged debt has murkier "would they have paid anyway?" disputes.
4. **Where they're equal:** universal pain, proven WTP, no-build MVP, one-founder testable. The tiebreakers above are all regulation/trust — which is exactly where a solo founder must avoid friction.

## STAGE F — FINAL DECISION

**1. #1 recommendation:** **"Recover" — lost-revenue win-back for high-ticket service SMBs.** A done-for-you concierge that re-engages a business's declined estimates, lapsed customers, and no-show slots with personalised, owner-approved follow-up; paid on contingency.

**2. #2 backup:** **AR collections-as-a-service** (only if you accept the licensing/trust friction; start B2B-only to dodge FDCPA, and check state registration first).

**3. Why #1 beats #2:** lower regulation (no debt-collector licensing/FDCPA), lower trust barrier (friendly win-back vs. chasing your client), cleaner contingency attribution — while matching #2 on pain, WTP, no-build MVP, and speed. (Detail above.)

**4. First customer segment:** owner-operated, high-ticket, appointment/estimate-based service businesses, 1–5 locations, $500k–$5M revenue, with a PMS/POS holding the lost-revenue data — **beachhead: independent auto repair + optometry or aesthetic/med-spa.** *Avoid dental first (HIPAA).*

**5. First offer:** *"I'll win back revenue your front desk is too busy to chase — declined quotes and customers who drifted away. You pay me 20% of what actually comes back, and $0 if nothing does. No software to install."*

**6. Exact no-code/manual MVP:** Owner emails you a CSV export of declined estimates + lapsed customers. You draft personalised, owner-approved win-back messages (LLM-assisted), send from the business's channel with opt-out, book returns, and report recovered $. **Zero product. Zero code.** (Tools: a spreadsheet, an LLM chat, the owner's email/SMS, Calendly.)

**7. Outreach script (cold):**
> "Hi [NAME] — I help [auto repair / optometry] owners win back revenue from declined quotes and customers who drifted off. I'll run a free estimate of how much is recoverable from your own records, then go get it — you pay only a cut of what actually comes back. Worth a 10-minute look?"

**8. Discovery-call questions:** (a) When a quote is declined or a customer doesn't rebook, who follows up — and does it actually happen? (b) Roughly how many a month, and average ticket? (c) What have you tried (recall tool, VA, ISA) — cost and result? (d) If I recovered $X next month and you paid 20%, easy yes? (e) Any worry about me messaging your customers — what would make you comfortable? (f) Can you export those lists from your system?

**9. Free audit template:** "Recovery snapshot — [Business]: declined estimates (6 mo): n / $; lapsed customers: n / est. $ at [conservative %]; no-show slots (90d): n. **Conservative recoverable: $X/mo.** Pilot: 30 days, your-voice + you-approve messages, you pay 20% of recovered revenue, $0 if none." *(Method note: estimate uses [assumptions]; actuals measured in pilot.)*

**10. Pricing test:** Offer two side by side — **(A) pure contingency 20% of recovered revenue**; **(B) $299/mo + 12% of recovered.** Whichever ≥3 owners pick reveals the model. Pure contingency should win first (lowest risk to buyer).

**11. Kill thresholds:**
- < 2 serious buying conversations from 20 targeted owners → framing wrong (reframe).
- Avg recoverable < ~$2k/mo/shop across 3 free audits → economics weak (pivot beachhead).
- Across 2 paid pilots, effective return < your hourly floor with no automation path → reframe or pivot to backup.
- A vertical's lists lack lawful basis to message (TCPA) → drop that vertical.

**12. 7-day validation plan:**
- **Day 1:** pick 2 verticals; build a 30-owner list; finalise offer + scripts (file 21).
- **Days 2–3:** send 20 outbound; book conversations; offer the free audit.
- **Day 4:** run ≥3 free recovery audits from exports; quantify "$X recoverable."
- **Day 5:** convert ≥1–2 audits into paid contingency pilots (sign 1-page agreement — lawyer-reviewed).
- **Days 6–7:** manually run win-back on the first pilot list; log messages → replies → bookings → $ recovered → hours. Compute recovery rate. Decide: GREEN (scale + pick vertical) / REFRAME / pivot to #2.

**13. What NOT to build yet:** no software, dashboard, website (beyond a form), PMS integration, or mobile app; **no blockchain/smart contracts** (irrelevant here); don't start in dental (HIPAA); don't chase the voice-receptionist market; don't become a third-party debt collector.
